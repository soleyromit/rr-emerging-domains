## A. Positioning statement & key differentiators

**Positioning statement:** Prism is the system of record for clinical/experiential education — and ACPE's Standards 2025 + PHARMS transition is asking every pharmacy program to re-prove itself in the same cycle Prism's placement engine already covers most of that evidence for.

Only Maryland Baltimore and Belmont appear on both current lists. **Why pharmacy programs would consider switching:**

1. **The re-mapping work is happening regardless of vendor.** ACPE retired AACP's AAMS for its own PHARMS platform the same cycle Standards 2025 took effect — programs are re-building their evidence map either way. That's the moment switching cost looks smallest, not a moment created by a sales pitch.
2. **CORE's own users name the wedge.** CORE ELMS holds ~90% of the market and rates well overall (4.7/5, 4.9/5 service) — this is not a "the incumbent is bad" pitch. But its own reviewers call the reporting suite "lacking depth and consistency" and describe modules as "disjointed." Lead with cohesion and reporting depth, not a parity claim.
3. **Preceptor gating already exists, just pointed at the wrong entity.** Prism's shipped student-compliance gating (immunizations, background checks) is the same pattern ACPE needs applied to preceptors (license status, completed orientation) — a configuration, not a rebuild.
4. **The campus is usually already an Exxat campus.** 99 of the 144 grid-matched Pharmacy institutions in the CRM export already run Exxat for at least one other discipline (PT on 79 of them, OT on 53, PA on 49, Nursing on 44 — distinct institutions, not sheet rows). For two-thirds of the market this is an expansion inside an existing relationship, not a cold logo — the "one Exxat for the university" pitch the domain expert names, where pharmacy can pull the smaller PT/OT/PA programs' software choice with it.
5. **e-Value is the one pharmacy incumbent the domain expert treats as displaceable — with a caveat you must carry.** The session says "e-Value is archaic and they're not making any significant enhancements" and that "some pharmacy customers" still subscribe; this repo's own teardown of the merged MedHub/E*Value line finds an actively shipping product, but that evidence is GME-branded (ACGME, CLER, ADS), and the vendor's own /pharmacy/ URL names no ACPE or IPPE/APPE capability. Pitch the pharmacy SKU as under-invested, not the company — and confirm it on the call before leaning on it.

## C. Objection handling

**"We're already on CORE ELMS — why switch?"**
Don't contest the relationship — CORE earns its 4.9/5 service score. Ask what their last self-study or interim report actually cost them to assemble: CORE's own reviewers report a reporting suite that "lacks depth and consistency" and modules that feel "disjointed." That's the gap Prism's unified Courses/Faculty/Students architecture answers directly.

**"We just went through the PHARMS transition — bad timing to also switch platforms."**
Reframe: the re-mapping work is unavoidable regardless of vendor, because it's ACPE's system, not the incumbent's. This is the cycle where the evidence gets rebuilt either way — it's actually the lowest-switching-cost moment, not the worst one.

**"Does Prism actually cover ACPE-specific requirements, or is this generic?"**
Be honest about the one real gap first: Prism has no ACPE-standards-mapped evidence aggregator — the Accreditation Management pillar is a Q3 2027 roadmap item, not shipped, and CORE's CompMS module covers standards-mapping today, so don't claim parity there. Everything else that matters operationally is covered: the 2:1 preceptor ratio cap, the four required APPE settings, IPPE hour sub-thresholds, and site-level affiliation agreements — the shipped Contracts feature tracks signed agreements per location with expiry alerts (the one open question is whether an expiring contract blocks new scheduling or only flags it; `accreditation/acpe.yaml` 3.5.b, corrected 2026-09-10).

**"What does this cost?"**
No public pricing exists for either product at the pharmacy-specific tier — CORE's own pricing is confirmed quote-only. Don't improvise a number; route to whoever owns pricing for a real quote.

**"We're on e-Value, not CORE — does any of this apply to us?"**
More than to a CORE shop, but carry the nuance. The domain expert's read is that e-Value is "archaic," not being significantly enhanced, and still holds "some pharmacy customers" — the only pharmacy incumbent she treats as displaceable rather than defended. But this repo's teardown of the merged MedHub/E*Value line (`competitors/e-value.yaml`) finds shipping accreditation automation and standards-aligned curriculum mapping, so "they've stopped building" is not safe to say out loud. The reconciliation that fits both facts: the investment is visible on the GME side (ACGME NAS, CLER, ADS reporting), while the vendor's own /pharmacy/ page is a generic health-science page with no ACPE or IPPE/APPE mention. So ask, don't assert: "When did your pharmacy module last get a meaningful release?" — and let their answer make the case. If it does, the switching-cost frame is the same as for CORE (PHARMS + Standards 2025 re-mapping happens regardless), and the migration is off a smaller install base. Adjacent evidence that e-Value's footprint is shrinking (a peer-reviewed survey showing use falling from 44.8% to 28.6%) is from DO programs, not pharmacy — don't quote it as a pharmacy number.

**"Why would we buy a pharmacy system from a PT/OT vendor?"**
Because on most pharmacy campuses Exxat is already the university's vendor, not a stranger. The CRM export shows 99 of 144 grid-matched Pharmacy institutions already run Exxat for another discipline — PT on 79, OT on 53, PA on 49, Nursing on 44 (distinct institutions). All four pharmacy clients the export marks sit on such campuses (Maryland Baltimore: CCS, Nursing, PA, Social Work; Belmont: Medicine, Nursing, OT, PT; College of Southern Maryland: Nursing; PCOM: PT) — but see the rule at the top of §A: that client list is disputed and unreconciled (TODO), so use the campus-adjacency fact, not the client names, until it is. Lead with the shared-campus reference and the university-level contract the domain expert describes — and know that adjacency alone doesn't hold the account: Husson churned from pharmacy while its Nursing, OT and PT programs stayed on Exxat.

- Eliminate the standards-to-evidence crosswalk rebuild every accreditation cycle — the single most-cited pain from the Compliance/Accreditation Liaison role, repo-wide.
